Medicare Questions

The questions we hear most from people approaching 65 and those already enrolled.

Medicare Supplement works alongside Original Medicare — it pays the costs Medicare leaves behind (deductibles, co-pays, coinsurance) and allows you to see any doctor who accepts Medicare, nationwide. Medicare Advantage replaces Original Medicare with a private plan that typically includes drug coverage and extras but restricts you to a network. Neither is universally better — the right choice depends on your health, doctors, and how you use healthcare. We compare both for your specific situation.
Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 3 months before your 65th birthday, your birthday month, and the 3 months after. Enrolling late without qualifying employer coverage triggers permanent premium penalties — 10% per year for Part B. Call us before you turn 65 and we'll make sure you don't miss anything.

Supplemental Coverage

When supplemental makes sense and when it doesn't.

Even good health insurance has deductibles, co-pays, and out-of-pocket maximums. Supplemental coverage pays cash directly to you — which you can use for anything: your deductible, lost wages, or anything else. A critical illness plan pays a lump sum for a cancer, heart attack, or stroke diagnosis — the exact moment when income may stop and costs spike simultaneously. Whether it makes sense depends on your specific coverage gaps and financial cushion.
Yes — and many Medicare beneficiaries benefit significantly from hospital indemnity or cancer coverage, because Medicare's out-of-pocket costs can still be substantial for serious conditions.
