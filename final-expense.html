What Final Expense Covers

The real cost of losing
someone you love

The average funeral in the United States costs between $8,000 and $12,000. Add in any remaining medical bills, credit card balances, or personal loans — and the financial burden on your family can reach $20,000 or more.

Final expense insurance exists to remove that burden completely. Your family receives a tax-free lump sum, paid directly to your beneficiary — within days of a certified claim — to handle costs exactly as they choose.

The coverage is permanent. The rate never increases. And it never requires a medical exam to qualify.

Coverage Options

Three coverage paths.
One for every health situation.

Best Rates
Level Benefit

Full death benefit from day one. Available for applicants in reasonably good health. Typically the most affordable final expense option.

  • Full death benefit from day one
  • Best available premium rates
  • Health questions required
  • No medical exam
Graded Benefit
Modified Benefit

For applicants with more significant health history. A modified death benefit applies in the first 2–3 years, with full coverage after that period.

  • Accepts more health conditions
  • Partial benefit in first 2–3 years
  • Full benefit after graded period
  • No medical exam
Guaranteed Issue
Guaranteed Acceptance

No health questions. No exam. Cannot be declined. For applicants who have been declined elsewhere or have significant health concerns. Graded benefit in early years.

  • Cannot be declined for health reasons
  • No health questions whatsoever
  • Waiting period applies (typically 2 years)
  • Higher premium than underwritten plans

Final expense is a type of whole life insurance with lower coverage amounts (typically $5,000–$35,000) and simplified underwriting — no medical exam. It's specifically designed to cover end-of-life costs and tends to be more accessible to seniors, including those with health conditions that might disqualify them from traditional life insurance.
