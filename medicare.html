Plan Types

Three different Medicare paths.
One right answer for your situation.

Most Flexible
Medicare Supplement
Also called Medigap

Works alongside Original Medicare (Parts A & B) to cover the gaps — copays, coinsurance, and deductibles. See any doctor who accepts Medicare, anywhere in the country. Predictable out-of-pocket costs.

  • No network restrictions — use any Medicare provider
  • Predictable out-of-pocket maximum
  • Standardized plans — same benefits across carriers
  • Higher monthly premium than Advantage
Most Popular
Medicare Advantage
Part C — bundled coverage

An all-in-one alternative to Original Medicare. Includes Part A, Part B, and often Part D. Many plans include dental, vision, and hearing. Network-based. Lower premiums — often $0/month.

  • Often $0 monthly premium
  • Bundled medical, dental, vision, hearing
  • Coordinated care within a network
  • Out-of-pocket maximum protects against catastrophic costs
Prescription Coverage
Medicare Part D
Prescription drug coverage

Standalone prescription drug coverage added to Original Medicare or a Supplement plan. We check every plan's formulary against your specific medications before recommending anything.

  • Covers prescription drugs at the pharmacy
  • We compare your exact medications against each plan's formulary
  • Formularies and copays change annually — we review every year
  • Late enrollment penalties if you miss your window

Enrollment Periods

When you can enroll — and why timing matters

Initial Enrollment

Turning 65

The most important Medicare window. Begins 3 months before your 65th birthday month and ends 3 months after. Enroll here without penalties or medical underwriting.

Annual Enrollment Period

Oct 15 – Dec 7

Every year, you can switch Medicare Advantage plans, switch from Advantage to Original Medicare, or change Part D plans. Changes take effect January 1. We review every client annually.

Special Enrollment

Life Events

Losing employer coverage, moving to a new area, or other qualifying life events trigger a Special Enrollment Period. Call us as soon as you know — timing is critical to avoid gaps in coverage.
