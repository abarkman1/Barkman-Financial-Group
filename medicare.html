The Basics

Medicare has 4 parts.
Here's what each one does.

A
Hospital Insurance

Covers inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, hospice, and some home health services.

CostMost people pay $0 premium for Part A. Deductibles and copays apply per benefit period.
B
Medical Insurance

Covers doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services, medical equipment, and services outside a hospital.

CostStandard premium $174.70/mo (2024). 20% coinsurance after deductible — with no out-of-pocket maximum.
C
Medicare Advantage

A private plan that replaces Original Medicare. Usually includes prescription drugs and may include dental, vision, and hearing.

ImportantNetwork restrictions apply. Out-of-pocket maximums vary significantly by plan and carrier.
D
Drug Coverage

Prescription drug coverage. Added as a standalone plan with Original Medicare, or often included in Medicare Advantage.

CostVaries by plan and medications. A late enrollment penalty applies if you delay without creditable coverage.
The Big Decision

Advantage vs. Supplement —
what's the real difference?

This is the most important choice most new Medicare enrollees face. Here's what each one means for your everyday life.

🛡
Medicare Supplement
Also called Medigap
  • Works alongside Original Medicare — doesn't replace it
  • Pays your deductibles, co-pays, and 20% coinsurance
  • Use any doctor in the U.S. who accepts Medicare — no networks
  • No referrals required for specialists
  • Predictable monthly costs — few financial surprises
  • Separate Part D drug plan required
Best for: People who travel, see specialists frequently, or want maximum flexibility and predictable out-of-pocket costs.
📋
Medicare Advantage
Part C — replaces Original Medicare
  • Managed by a private insurer under Medicare contract
  • Usually includes prescription drug coverage (Part D)
  • Often includes dental, vision, and hearing extras
  • Typically lower monthly premiums than Supplements
  • Network restrictions — must use in-network providers
  • Higher potential out-of-pocket when serious care is needed
Best for: People who stay local, are generally healthy, and want lower premiums with added benefits bundled in.

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on your health, your doctors, your medications, and how you use healthcare. That's exactly what we help you figure out — at no cost.

Enrollment Windows

Timing matters.
Here's when you can act.

Medicare has strict enrollment periods. Missing them doesn't just mean waiting — it can mean permanent cost increases. Call us before you turn 65.

For New Enrollees
Initial Enrollment Period
3 months before · birth month · 3 months after 65th birthday

Your first and most important window. Enroll in Parts A and B without penalty. Call us before you turn 65 — not after.

Every Year
Annual Enrollment Period
October 15 – December 7

Switch, drop, or join a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan. Changes take effect January 1. We review your plan every year during this window.

Early Year
Open Enrollment Period
January 1 – March 31

If you have Medicare Advantage, you can switch to a different Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare during this window.

⚠️

Late enrollment penalties are permanent

If you miss your Part B enrollment window without qualifying employer coverage, your premium can increase 10% for each 12-month period you delayed — and that increase stays with you permanently. Part D has a similar penalty. Call us before your 65th birthday and we'll make sure you don't miss anything.
