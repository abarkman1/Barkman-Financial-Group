What Is an Annuity

A contract that turns savings
into guaranteed income

An annuity is a contract between you and an insurance company. You contribute a lump sum or series of payments. In return, the insurance company provides guaranteed income payments — starting immediately or at a future date you choose.

The core problem annuities solve: You can plan for retirement. You cannot plan for how long retirement will last. An annuity removes longevity risk — the risk of outliving your money — from the equation entirely.

Annuities are not one-size-fits-all. The right structure depends on your timeline, risk tolerance, income needs, and estate goals. That's where we come in.

Annuity Types

Three structures.
One right answer for your situation.

No single annuity type is universally best. The right structure depends on your risk tolerance, timeline, and income goals.

Lowest Risk
Fixed Annuity
Predictable. Protected. Simple.

The insurance company guarantees a specific interest rate for a set period. Your principal and growth are fully protected from market volatility.

  • Guaranteed fixed interest rate
  • Principal fully protected
  • No market exposure
  • Predictable income stream
  • Simple structure — easy to understand
  • Growth limited to guaranteed rate
  • Surrender charges if withdrawn early
Balanced
Fixed Indexed Annuity
Market upside. Zero downside.

Growth is linked to the performance of a market index (like the S&P 500) up to a cap rate — but you can never lose principal due to market decline. The best of both worlds for many retirees.

  • Principal protected from market loss
  • Growth potential tied to market index
  • Floor of 0% — you never lose to the market
  • Participation rates and caps vary by product
  • Lifetime income rider options available
  • Gains capped at participation/cap rate
Growth Focused
Variable Annuity
Maximum growth potential.

Your money is invested in sub-accounts similar to mutual funds. Returns vary with market performance. Higher potential return — and higher potential risk.

  • Full market participation — upside and downside
  • Wide range of investment sub-account options
  • Tax-deferred growth
  • Optional living benefit riders available
  • Death benefit options available
  • Principal at risk from market decline
  • Higher fees than fixed products
How Annuities Work

Two phases. One lifetime of income.

01
Accumulation Phase

Your money grows

You contribute funds — either as a lump sum or over time. Your money grows tax-deferred during the accumulation phase. You choose how long this phase lasts, from a few years to decades. No taxes are owed on gains until you begin withdrawals.

02
Distribution Phase

Your income flows

You begin receiving income payments. You choose the structure: a fixed period, a specific dollar amount, or income for the rest of your life — no matter how long you live. Income can cover just you, or you and a spouse jointly.

Who Benefits Most

Annuities make the most sense in these situations

🎯

Approaching Retirement

Within 5–10 years of retirement, protecting what you've saved becomes as important as growing it. A fixed or fixed indexed annuity can lock in a portion of your assets against market risk during this critical window.

♾

Concerned About Outliving Savings

If you don't have a pension, Social Security alone may not cover your expenses. A lifetime income annuity provides the pension-like income stream your retirement needs — guaranteed for as long as you live.

📊

Looking for Tax-Deferred Growth

If you've maxed out your 401(k) and IRA contributions, annuities offer another tax-deferred growth vehicle with no annual contribution limits. Your money grows without being reduced by taxes until you withdraw.

🛡

Want Market Participation Without Risk

A fixed indexed annuity lets you participate in index growth during bull markets while your principal is protected from loss. For risk-averse savers who don't want to miss market gains, this is a powerful combination.

💑

Planning for a Spouse

A joint and survivor annuity continues income payments to your spouse after your passing. For couples where one spouse has significantly lower retirement savings, this provides essential financial security.

🏦

Estate Planning Needs

Many annuities include death benefits that pass to named beneficiaries outside of probate — with more control and flexibility than many other estate transfer vehicles.
